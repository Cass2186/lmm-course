1 · Why mixed models?

Pseudoreplication, lost power, and the intraclass correlation

Course data (loads automatically; click to see the code)

R runs inside your browser. The first visit downloads R and the packages, which takes 20–40 seconds; after that the browser caches them. Wait for this block to finish before running exercises.

Every row in a hypertrophy dataset belongs to someone. Three ultrasound images from one visit are not three participants, and a participant’s week-12 CSA is not independent of their week-0 CSA. lm() assumes every row is independent. Get that wrong in one direction and your p-values are far too small (pseudoreplication). Get it wrong in the other and you throw away the within-person information that makes a repeated-measures trial powerful.

Levels and units

Level Unit n here What varies
4 Arm 2 Treatment (randomized)
3 Participant 40 Sex, baseline size, individual response
2 Visit 115 Week, day-to-day hydration, probe placement
1 Image 344 Image-to-image measurement error

The independent unit for a treatment effect is the level at which treatment was randomized: the participant. You have 40 of those, not 344.

Explore: what pseudoreplication does

Each simulated experiment below has no true group difference. It compares two groups with a t-test on every image (pseudoreplication) and with a t-test on participant means (correct). Move the sliders to see how often each test gives a false positive; the result updates each time.

viewof n_per = Inputs.range([4, 20], {value: 10, step: 1, label: "Participants per group"})
viewof k_img = Inputs.range([1, 10], {value: 3, step: 1, label: "Images per participant"})
viewof icc_target = Inputs.range([0, 0.99], {value: 0.9, step: 0.01, label: "ICC"})

With 1 image per participant the two tests agree. The higher the ICC and the more images, the worse the image-level test gets, because extra images of the same muscle add almost no new information.

Q1 — Know your data

How many visits are there per arm and week, and which participants are missing a visit?

Count by Week in the first line. Everyone should have 3 visits, so keep rows with n < 3.

sess |> count(Group, Week)
sess |> count(ID) |> filter(n < 3)
csa  |> filter(n_img < 3)      # the visit with a missing image

Placebo has 20 / 19 / 20 visits and Protein 20 / 19 / 17 at weeks 0 / 6 / 12. P12 and P26 missed week 6; P07, P18 and P33 have no week-12 visit. P04’s week-6 visit has 2 images. A complete-case repeated-measures ANOVA would drop all 5 of these participants.

Q2 — Spaghetti plot

Plot VL CSA against week, one line per participant, faceted by arm, with the arm mean overlaid.

Lines are grouped by participant (ID) and faceted by arm (Group).

ggplot(csa, aes(Week, VL_CSA_cm2)) +
  geom_line(aes(group = ID, colour = Sex), alpha = 0.5) +
  stat_summary(fun = mean, geom = "line", linewidth = 1.2) +
  facet_wrap(~ Group) +
  scale_x_continuous(breaks = c(0, 6, 12)) +
  theme_bw()

People start anywhere from about 15 to 33 cm² (men about 23–33, women about 15–27), but almost every line rises gently and nearly in parallel by 1–2 cm². The treatment question is about the slopes, which are small compared with the vertical spread between people.

Q3 — Three analyses of one question

The trial question is the GroupProtein:Week_f12 coefficient: extra change to week 12 with Protein. Fit it three ways and compare estimates and SEs for that term and for GroupProtein (the baseline difference).

The first model uses the image-level data frame img. The random intercept is for participant, ID.

f_img <- lm(VL_CSA_cm2 ~ Group * Week_f + Sex, data = img)
f_vis <- lm(VL_CSA_cm2 ~ Group * Week_f + Sex, data = csa)
f_lmm <- lmer(VL_CSA_cm2 ~ Group * Week_f + Sex + (1 | ID), data = csa)
Model Baseline diff (SE) Diff in change (SE)
lm, 344 images 0.902 (0.538) 0.701 (0.777)
lm, 115 visits 0.902 (0.946) 0.701 (1.367)
lmer 0.902 (0.948) 0.990 (0.282)

The image-level lm SE for the baseline difference is too small because it counts every image as a new person (pseudoreplication). For the difference in change, both lm fits are 3–5× less precise than the LMM, because the ~3 cm² between-person spread sits in their residual. The estimate also shifts (0.70 vs 0.99), because with dropout the unpaired lm compares different people at week 0 and week 12.

Q4 — ICC

Fit an intercept-only random-intercept model to csa and compute the ICC.

The function is icc().

performance::icc(fit0)

Participant SD ≈ 4.04 cm², residual SD ≈ 1.00 cm², ICC ≈ 0.94. In plain language: 94% of the variation in CSA values is people being different sizes; only 6% is change within people plus measurement noise. Each extra participant is worth far more than each extra scan of the same participant.
