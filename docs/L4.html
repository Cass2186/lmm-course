4 · Inference and diagnostics

p-values, confidence intervals, residual checks, log outcomes and MMRM

Course data (loads automatically; click to see the code)

R runs inside your browser. The first visit downloads R and the packages, which takes 20–40 seconds; after that the browser caches them. Wait for this block to finish before running exercises.

For Gaussian mixed models there is no exact denominator df, so there are several reasonable ways to get a p-value:

Method How Notes
Satterthwaite summary(fit), anova(fit) lmerTest default
Kenward–Roger anova(fit, ddf = "Kenward-Roger"), emmeans Best small-sample behaviour; slower
Likelihood ratio anova(m0, m1) on ML fits Tests groups of terms
Parametric bootstrap confint(fit, method = "boot") Slow, fewest assumptions

With ~40 participants they usually agree to the second decimal. Pick one in the analysis plan.

A random intercept implies compound symmetry: every pair of visits is equally correlated. An MMRM drops the random effects and gives the residuals an unstructured covariance instead. That’s the standard primary analysis for regulatory trials with fixed visits.

Explore: raw or log scale?

Choose an outcome and a scale. A fan shape in the residuals, or a bigger residual SD in men than women, means variability grows with the size of the outcome, and a log scale is usually the fix.

viewof diag_outcome = Inputs.select(
  new Map([["Leg press 1RM (kg)", "Leg_Press_1RM_kg"], ["Leg lean mass (kg)", "Leg_Lean_Mass_kg"], ["VL CSA (cm²)", "VL_CSA_cm2"]]),
  {label: "Outcome"})
viewof diag_log = Inputs.radio(["raw", "log"], {value: "raw", label: "Scale"})

For 1RM, the raw-scale residual SD is ≈ 10 kg in women vs ≈ 14 kg in men; on the log scale both are ≈ 0.05 (≈ 5% day-to-day variation). Lean mass and CSA look fine on either scale.

Exercises

All exercises use the primary model fit <- lmer(VL_CSA_cm2 ~ Group * Week_f + Sex + (1 | ID), data = csa), which is created for you:

Q1 — Three routes to a p-value

Use ddf = "Kenward-Roger". Satterthwaite: t ≈ 3.51 on ≈ 71 df, p ≈ 0.00079; Kenward–Roger is essentially identical. The LRT for the whole interaction gives χ²(2) ≈ 12.1, p ≈ 0.0024, vs the F-test’s p ≈ 0.0033. The LRT is a little optimistic because it treats variance parameters as known; with 10 participants the difference would matter.

Q2 — Three kinds of CI

The bootstrap takes about 15 seconds in the browser.

Wald 0.437 to 1.543; profile 0.444 to 1.535; parametric bootstrap ≈ 0.41 to 1.50 with set.seed(1) and 200 simulations (it varies with the seed and nsim; use ≥ 1000 for a paper). emmeans with KR df gives 0.427 to 1.553. A warning that a few bootstrap refits didn’t converge is normal.

Q3 — Leave one participant out

Refit the model 40 times, each time without one participant. Does anyone drive the result?

Filter with ID != id. The estimate ranges from ≈ 0.87 (dropping P25) to ≈ 1.13 (dropping P21), so no single participant drives the result.

Q4 — Log-scale 1RM as % change

type = "response" back-transforms to ratios. Week-12 gains: Placebo ×1.30 (+30%), Protein ×1.33 (+33%). Ratio of fold-changes 1.026 (95% CI 0.968 to 1.087): no evidence that protein affected strength, and the CI rules out a large effect.

Q5 — MMRM vs random intercept

CS vs unstructured: χ²(4) ≈ 8.98, p ≈ 0.062. The unstructured correlations fall with distance between visits (0.97, 0.96 for adjacent visits; 0.94 for week 0 vs 12), which is what individual growth rates produce. The MMRM week-12 effect is 0.993 (SE 0.336) vs 0.990 (0.282) under compound symmetry. The random-intercept SE is too small when the week-0/week-12 correlation is lower than CS assumes, which is the argument for prespecifying an MMRM (or a random slope).
